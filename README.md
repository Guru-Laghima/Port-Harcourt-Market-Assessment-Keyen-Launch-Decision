# Port Harcourt Market Assessment – Keyen Launch Decision

## Project Title
Port Harcourt Ride-Hailing & Multi-Service Market Assessment  
Launch Decision Report for Keyen (Private Rides + Food Delivery + Package/Dispatch)

## Project Objectives
- To evaluate the commercial and operational viability of launching a ride-hailing platform (Keyen) in Port Harcourt.
- To provide a clear Go / No-Go recommendation based on primary market research, competitor analysis, unit-economics modelling, and regulatory review.
- To assess additional service verticals (food delivery, package/dispatch, and shared rides) as potential second offerings.
- To define a realistic, low-risk pilot structure with clear success criteria and cost estimates.

## Dataset Used
- Primary market research and competitor analysis
- Unit-economics modelling (supporting Excel workbook)
- Regulatory and operational review (Rivers State context)
- Secondary public data on population, fares, commissions, and local transport dynamics  
Data period: 2025–2026

## Key Questions Explored
1. Is Port Harcourt a viable launch market for Keyen?
2. What is the size and nature of demand (especially high-value corridors)?
3. How intense is competition from Bolt, inDrive, and local players?
4. What are the driver economics and supply challenges?
5. What are the realistic launch costs for a 3-month and 6-month pilot?
6. What regulatory and operational risks exist in Rivers State?
7. Should Keyen also launch food delivery and package/dispatch services?
8. What is the optimal pilot design and success criteria?

## Process
1. **Market & Demand Analysis**  
   Assessed urban population (~3.8–4.0 million), demand concentration (GRA, Trans-Amadi, Airport, UNIPORT), and willingness-to-pay segments.

2. **Competitor & Driver Analysis**  
   Mapped positioning of Bolt, inDrive, and SimpliRide. Analysed commission structures, fare levels, multi-homing behaviour, and driver pain points.

3. **Unit Economics & Cost Modelling**  
   Built lean, base, and aggressive launch-cost scenarios for 3-month and 6-month horizons.

4. **Regulatory Review**  
   Examined Rivers State levies, informal tickets, AUATON dynamics, and standard compliance requirements.

5. **Multi-Service Assessment**  
   Evaluated food delivery, package/dispatch, and shared rides as potential additional verticals.

## Report Structure
**Main Report**
- Executive Assessment (Clear Recommendation)

- Final Clear Assessment

**Addendum**

## Key Insights & Recommendation

**Overall Recommendation: PROCEED TO PILOT (CONDITIONAL GO)**

| Dimension                  | Assessment                                                                 |
|---------------------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Market size & demand      | Sufficient – concentrated high-value demand in GRA, Trans-Amadi, Airport   |
| Competition intensity     | Moderate – less saturated than Lagos                                       |
| Driver supply & economics | Challenging but addressable with better take-rate                          |
| Unit economics            | Viable if take rate ≤12–15% and incentives well managed                    |
| Regulatory risk           | Material – requires proactive local engagement                             |
| Launch cost (Base case)   | ₦44.5m (3 months) / ₦86.4m (6 months)                                      |

**Critical Conditions for Success**
- Target effective take rate of 10–12% (or hybrid subscription model)
- Launch only in core corridors (GRA + Airport + Trans-Amadi + selected estates)
- Begin regulatory and AUATON engagement immediately
- Prioritise corporate and estate accounts
- Define clear 8–12 week pilot KPIs and a pre-agreed kill-switch

**Multi-Service Priority**
1. Private Ride-Hailing (Core)
2. Package/Dispatch and Food Delivery (High – can share the same driver pool)
3. Shared Rides (Later – only after density is proven)

## Conclusion
Port Harcourt presents a genuine commercial opportunity as an alternative or secondary launch market for Keyen. The combination of concentrated high-value demand, moderate competition, and visible driver dissatisfaction creates a realistic path to share — provided the pilot is tightly scoped, driver economics are competitive, and local regulatory engagement is prioritised from day one.

The evidence supports a **conditional GO** for a focused 8–12 week pilot. If the stated conditions on economics, geography, and regulatory engagement cannot be met, the market should be deferred.
